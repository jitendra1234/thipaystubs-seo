An **Employer Identification Number (EIN)** is a nine-digit federal tax ID for a business, written like 12-3456789. You need one to hire employees, run payroll, open many business bank accounts and file business tax returns. The good news: it is **free** from the IRS, and for most businesses it takes only a few minutes.

## Who needs an EIN

You generally need an EIN if your business:

- has **employees**, or will hire them;
- is a **corporation** or **partnership**;
- is a **multi-member LLC**, or a single-member LLC with employees or excise tax obligations;
- files employment, excise, or alcohol, tobacco and firearms tax returns;
- withholds tax on income paid to a nonresident alien;
- is a **trust**, **estate** or **nonprofit**.

Sole proprietors with no employees can often use their Social Security number, but many get an EIN anyway so they don't have to give their SSN to every client on a [Form W-9](/w9-form).

## Ways to apply

1. **Online at IRS.gov.** The fastest way. You answer the same questions as Form SS-4 and get your EIN at the end of the session. The responsible party must have a valid SSN, ITIN or EIN, and the IRS limits online applications to one EIN per responsible party per day.
2. **By fax.** Fax a completed Form SS-4 to the IRS. If you include a return fax number, you usually get the EIN back within about four business days.
3. **By mail.** Mail Form SS-4 to the IRS. This takes several weeks.
4. **International applicants** without a U.S. address can apply by phone.

## What Form SS-4 asks

- **Lines 1–6:** the legal name, trade name, mailing and street address, and the county and state where the business is located.
- **Lines 7a–7b:** the **responsible party**, the person who ultimately owns or controls the entity, and their SSN, ITIN or EIN.
- **Lines 8a–9b:** whether it is an LLC (and how many members), and the type of entity: sole proprietor, partnership, corporation, nonprofit, trust, estate and so on.
- **Line 10:** why you are applying: started a new business, hired employees, banking purposes and more.
- **Lines 11–15:** start date, the closing month of your accounting year, how many employees you expect, and when you will first pay wages.
- **Lines 16–17:** your principal business activity and what you sell or do.
- **Line 18:** whether you have applied for an EIN before.

If you expect to owe **$1,000 or less** in employment taxes for the year, line 14 lets you ask to file Form 944 once a year instead of Form 941 every quarter.

## Watch out for paid "EIN services"

Many websites look official but charge a fee to "file" for an EIN. The IRS never charges for an EIN. Apply directly at IRS.gov, or by fax or mail with Form SS-4.

## After you get your EIN

- Keep the IRS confirmation letter (CP 575) with your records.
- Use the EIN on payroll forms such as [Form 941](/941-form), [W-2](/w2-form) and [W-3](/w3-form), and on [pay stubs](/create-pay-stub) for your employees.
- Give it to clients on Form W-9 instead of your SSN.
