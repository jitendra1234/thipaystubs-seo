If you freelance, drive for a delivery app, or run a one-person business, the IRS treats you as self-employed. Instead of a W-2, you report what you earned and spent on **Schedule C (Form 1040), Profit or Loss From Business**. This guide walks through how it works in plain English.

## Who files Schedule C

You generally file Schedule C if you are a **sole proprietor**, an **independent contractor** paid on a 1099-NEC, or the owner of a **single-member LLC** that has not chosen to be taxed as a corporation. If you run more than one business, you file a separate Schedule C for each.

## How Schedule C is laid out

**The top section (A–J)** describes the business: what you do, a six-digit business code, your business name and EIN if you have one, your accounting method (most small businesses use cash), and whether you materially participated.

**Part I, income:**

- **Line 1:** gross receipts or sales, everything customers paid you, including amounts reported on 1099-NEC and 1099-K forms.
- **Line 2:** returns and allowances.
- **Line 4:** cost of goods sold, if you sell products.
- **Line 7:** gross income.

**Part II, expenses (lines 8–27):** advertising, car and truck expenses, contract labor, insurance, legal and professional fees, office expense, rent, supplies, travel, deductible meals, utilities, wages and more.

**Lines 28–31:** total expenses, tentative profit, the deduction for business use of your home, and finally your **net profit or loss**.

## Common deductions people miss

- **Home office:** if you use part of your home regularly and only for business, you can deduct it. The simplified method allows $5 per square foot, up to 300 square feet.
- **Vehicle use:** either actual expenses or the IRS standard mileage rate for business miles. Keep a mileage log.
- **Software, phone and internet:** the business-use share is deductible.
- **Contractors you pay:** goes on line 11, and you may need to send them a [1099-NEC](/1099-nec-form).

Keep receipts and records. Every deduction must be an ordinary and necessary expense for your business.

## A worked example

Maria is a freelance graphic designer.

| Line | Amount |
| --- | --- |
| 1 Gross receipts | $62,000 |
| 8 Advertising | $1,200 |
| 18 Office expense | $900 |
| 22 Supplies | $600 |
| 27b Other (software) | $1,300 |
| 28 Total expenses | $4,000 |
| 29 Tentative profit | $58,000 |
| 30 Home office (200 sq ft × $5) | $1,000 |
| **31 Net profit** | **$57,000** |

## What happens after Schedule C

- **Income tax:** the net profit goes to [Schedule 1](/schedule-1-form), line 3, and then to Form 1040.
- **Self-employment tax:** if your net earnings are $400 or more, you also file Schedule SE. Self-employment tax covers Social Security and Medicare at a combined 15.3% on 92.35% of net earnings, with the Social Security part capped at the yearly wage base. Half of it is deductible on Schedule 1, line 15.
- **Estimated taxes:** with no employer withholding, most self-employed people pay quarterly with [Form 1040-ES](/1040-es-form) to avoid underpayment penalties.
- **QBI deduction:** many sole proprietors can also deduct up to 20% of qualified business income using Form 8995.
