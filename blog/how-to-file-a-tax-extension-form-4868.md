Running short on time before the April deadline? **Form 4868, Application for Automatic Extension of Time To File U.S. Individual Income Tax Return**, gives most taxpayers six more months to file. It is free, it is automatic, and you don't need to give a reason. But there is one big catch.

## The catch: an extension to file is not an extension to pay

Form 4868 moves your **filing** deadline, usually from April 15 to **October 15**. It does **not** move your **payment** deadline. Any tax you owe is still due in April. If you pay late, interest and a late-payment penalty apply to the unpaid amount, even with a valid extension.

That is why the form asks you to estimate your tax for the year.

## How to fill in Form 4868

The form is short:

**Part I, identification**
- **Line 1:** your name and address (include your spouse if you will file jointly).
- **Line 2:** your Social Security number.
- **Line 3:** your spouse's Social Security number.

**Part II, individual income tax**
- **Line 4:** your best estimate of your total tax for the year.
- **Line 5:** total payments already made, such as withholding from your pay and estimated tax payments.
- **Line 6:** balance due, line 4 minus line 5.
- **Line 7:** the amount you are paying with the extension.
- **Lines 8 and 9:** check boxes if you are out of the country, or if you file Form 1040-NR without wages subject to withholding.

### Example

Your estimated tax is $9,400 and $7,900 was withheld from your paychecks. Your balance due is **$1,500**. Paying that $1,500 by April avoids late-payment penalties, and you still have until October to finish your return.

## Ways to file the extension

- **E-file Form 4868** through tax software or IRS Free File.
- **Pay electronically and say it is for an extension.** When you pay through IRS Direct Pay, EFTPS or by card and choose the extension option, the IRS treats it as your extension. You don't need to send a separate form.
- **Mail a paper Form 4868** by the April deadline to the address in the instructions.

## Penalties to avoid

- **Failure to file:** usually 5% of the unpaid tax for each month or part of a month the return is late, up to 25%. A filed extension avoids this.
- **Failure to pay:** usually 0.5% of the unpaid tax per month, up to 25%, plus interest. An extension does not stop this, so pay what you can by April.

## Special situations

- **Out of the country:** U.S. citizens and residents living and working abroad on the regular due date generally get an automatic two-month extension to June 15, and can use Form 4868 for more time.
- **Disaster areas:** the IRS often postpones deadlines for people in federally declared disaster areas.
- **State returns:** many states have their own extension rules. Check your state's tax agency.
