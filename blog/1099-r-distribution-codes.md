Form 1099-R reports money paid out of pensions, annuities, retirement plans, IRAs and some insurance contracts. Most of the form is straightforward: how much was paid, how much is taxable and how much tax was withheld. The part that confuses people is **box 7, the distribution code**. That short code tells the IRS what kind of payout it was, and it can decide whether an extra tax applies.

## Where the code goes

Box 7 holds one or two characters. Next to it is a checkbox marked **IRA/SEP/SIMPLE**, which is ticked when the money came from one of those accounts. Some combinations of two codes are allowed, for example **7** and **B** together.

## The most common codes

| Code | What it means |
| --- | --- |
| **1** | Early distribution, no known exception. The person is usually under age 59½, and a 10% additional tax may apply. |
| **2** | Early distribution, exception applies. Under 59½, but an exception (such as separation from service in or after the year you turn 55) means the 10% additional tax does not apply. |
| **3** | Disability. |
| **4** | Death. Paid to a beneficiary or estate. |
| **7** | Normal distribution. Usually age 59½ or older. |
| **8** | Excess contributions plus earnings, taxable in the current year. |
| **B** | Distribution from a designated Roth account in an employer plan, such as a Roth 401(k). |
| **G** | Direct rollover to another qualified plan or a traditional IRA. Usually not taxable. |
| **H** | Direct rollover of a designated Roth account to a Roth IRA. |
| **J** | Early distribution from a Roth IRA. |
| **L** | Loan treated as a distribution, for example an unpaid 401(k) loan. |
| **Q** | Qualified distribution from a Roth IRA. Generally tax-free. |
| **T** | Roth IRA distribution, exception applies. |

There are other codes for less common situations, such as corrective distributions and annuity payments. The IRS instructions for Forms 1099-R and 5498 list them all.

## Why the code matters

- **Taxes:** a code **G** rollover is usually not taxable, while a code **7** distribution normally is.
- **The 10% additional tax:** code **1** tells the IRS that the 10% additional tax on early distributions may apply, unless you claim an exception on Form 5329.
- **Roth accounts:** codes **Q**, **T**, **J**, **B** and **H** help separate tax-free Roth withdrawals from taxable ones.

If you think the code on your form is wrong, contact the payer and ask for a corrected 1099-R before you file.

## Other boxes worth checking

- **Box 1, gross distribution:** the total paid out.
- **Box 2a, taxable amount:** the part that is taxable, if the payer knows it. If **box 2b "taxable amount not determined"** is ticked, you work it out yourself.
- **Box 4, federal income tax withheld:** carried to your tax return as a payment.
- **Boxes 14–19:** state and local tax withheld and distributions.

## When 1099-R forms are due

Payers generally send Copy B to the recipient by **January 31**, and file with the IRS by **February 28** on paper or **March 31** electronically. A 1099-R is usually required for distributions of $10 or more.
