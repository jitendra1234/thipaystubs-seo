If you pay employees, you probably know about Form 941 for quarterly payroll taxes. Form 940 is its annual cousin: the return for **federal unemployment tax**, better known as FUTA. It is paid only by the employer, never withheld from an employee's pay, and for most small businesses it comes to a modest amount per employee. Here is how it works.

## What FUTA tax is

The Federal Unemployment Tax Act funds the federal share of unemployment benefits. Employers pay FUTA tax on the wages they pay, and report it once a year on Form 940. Employees do not pay FUTA, so it never appears as a deduction on a pay stub.

## Who has to file Form 940

Most employers must file Form 940 if, in the current or previous year, either:

- they paid **$1,500 or more in wages** in any calendar quarter, or
- they had **at least one employee for some part of a day in 20 or more different weeks**.

Household employers (for example, paying a nanny) and agricultural employers have different thresholds, and household employers usually report FUTA on Schedule H with their personal return instead.

## How FUTA tax is calculated

The FUTA rate is **6.0%** on the first **$7,000** paid to each employee in the year. That $7,000 is called the FUTA wage base.

Employers that pay their state unemployment tax in full and on time usually get a **credit of up to 5.4%**, which brings the effective rate down to **0.6%**. That works out to at most **$42 per employee per year** (0.6% × $7,000).

The credit can be smaller if your state has borrowed from the federal government to pay unemployment benefits and has not repaid it. These are called **credit reduction states**, and the IRS publishes the list each year. If you paid wages in one, you complete Schedule A and pay a little more.

## A worked example

A small business pays three employees during the year:

| Employee | Wages for the year | Wages over $7,000 |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A | $48,000 | $41,000 |
| B | $30,000 | $23,000 |
| C | $5,000 | $0 |
| **Total** | **$83,000** | **$64,000** |

- **Line 3, total payments:** $83,000
- **Line 5, payments over $7,000 per employee:** $64,000
- **Line 7, taxable FUTA wages:** $83,000 − $64,000 = **$19,000**
- **Line 8, FUTA tax (× 0.6%):** **$114.00**

Employee C only earned $5,000, so all of their pay is taxable. Employees A and B are capped at $7,000 each.

## When to deposit FUTA tax

You don't always pay FUTA once a year. Work out your FUTA tax at the end of each quarter:

- If the amount owed for the quarter (plus any amount carried forward) is **more than $500**, deposit it by the last day of the month after the quarter ends.
- If it is **$500 or less**, carry it forward to the next quarter.

Deposits are made electronically, for example through EFTPS. Many small employers with only a few staff never go over $500 and simply pay with the return.

## When Form 940 is due

Form 940 for a year is due by **January 31** of the following year. If you deposited all of your FUTA tax on time, you get until **February 10**.

## Common mistakes

- **Forgetting the $7,000 cap.** FUTA is only charged on the first $7,000 per employee, not on all wages.
- **Counting exempt payments as taxable.** Some payments, such as certain retirement plan contributions and some fringe benefits, are exempt from FUTA and go on line 4.
- **Missing a credit reduction.** Check the IRS list if you paid wages in more than one state.
- **Mixing up Form 940 and 941.** Form 941 covers income tax withholding, Social Security and Medicare each quarter. Form 940 covers only FUTA, once a year.
